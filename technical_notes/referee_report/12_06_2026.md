# Referee-Style Report: "Quantity-Space Hyperprior Specification for Bayesian Hierarchical Models" (June 12, 2026 build)

Full top-to-bottom read of the compiled 103-page PDF. Four rendering bugs
found during the read were fixed immediately (commit 56ff647): the
main-text sensitivity table missing its non-baseline rows, three
appendix-only citations missing from References (per-appendix
refsections), the "7.1 *" Code-and-Data heading, and a "Figure ??" in
Appendix D. The report below covers the substance.

## Summary of the paper

The paper proposes specifying hierarchical priors on predictive
quantities (certainty equivalents, choice probabilities) rather than on
structural parameters, mapped to parameter space by an exact
Jacobian/Gram correction, with the hierarchical layer organized by a
two-parameter (alpha, kappa) variance decomposition. Applied to three
RDU models sharing CRRA utility (Prelec, Goldstein-Einhorn, Power PWFs)
on Harrison-Ng (2016) data, with cross-model CE comparisons and Bayes
factors under the shared prior.

## Overall assessment

Clear contribution, well-motivated bridge between predictive elicitation
and hierarchical prior construction, carefully executed application, and
unusually transparent diagnostics. The injectivity appendix is rigorous
and the marginal likelihood machinery is validated to a standard rarely
seen. Recommendation as a referee: revise and resubmit with one
substantive issue addressed (prior normalization in the Bayes factor
section) and a set of smaller clarifications.

## Major comments

1. PRIOR NORMALIZATION AND THE BAYES FACTORS (the one real issue).
   The induced prior is p(theta) proportional to p_Y(g(theta)) vol(J).
   Footnote 4 correctly notes that for k > p this density is not
   automatically normalized, and the Appendix C discussion notes prior
   mass outside the image is unreachable. But Section 5.4 does not
   engage with the consequence: the per-subject prior mass
   C_m(psi) = integral of the unnormalized induced prior need not equal
   1, generally differs ACROSS models (especially Model 3, whose
   quantity-space support is a 3-dimensional manifold), and may vary
   with psi. The computed log marginal likelihoods then contain a
   contamination term related to E[ sum_i log C_m(psi) ], which does not
   cancel between models. The claim that "all three models express
   identical prior beliefs about observables" is also literally false
   for Model 3, which cannot match the 4-dimensional direct prior on its
   lower-dimensional image; arguably that restriction is exactly what
   the BF should punish, but then the text should say so explicitly.
   Suggested fixes: (i) estimate C_m(psi) per model at representative
   posterior psi values (the existing importance-sampling machinery does
   this by setting the likelihood to 1) and either correct the log MLs
   or show C_m is near 1 (Models 1-2) and report the Model 3 constant;
   (ii) add a paragraph to 5.4 stating precisely what is shared (the
   quantity-space density) and what is not (its normalization over each
   model's feasible image). Given log BFs of 16.6/171.7/155.1, the
   ordering will almost surely survive, but the levels need this.

2. CONVERGENCE DIAGNOSTICS NEED ONE MORE SENTENCE OF QUANTIFICATION.
   Table E.1 reports max R-hat of 6.76/6.51/8.94 with medians near 1.02.
   The text attributes the tail to genuinely multimodal lambda for some
   subjects, consistent with Figure 3. A referee will want: the share of
   parameters with R-hat above 1.1 (or 1.05), confirmation that all
   HYPERparameter R-hats are near 1, and one sentence noting that the
   marginal likelihood estimator's validity does not depend on
   subject-level mixing (posterior draws enter only as proposal-fitting
   inputs on the subject side; the bridge route uses hyperparameter
   draws, which are well mixed). Note also that for genuinely multimodal
   subject posteriors, split R-hat overstates non-convergence but
   per-chain mode weights may still be unreliable; the cross-model CE
   conclusions do not hinge on those subjects, and saying so would
   preempt the question.

3. GE INJECTIVITY SHOULD BE EXPLICIT, NOT BY REMARK.
   Lemma B.8 proves Prelec two-point injectivity and remarks that GE
   follows "by the same argument"; Section B.2.2 says bijectivity is
   "likely available" for GE. The GE case is in fact a three-line
   argument (logit of w is affine in (log delta, gamma) with regressors
   (1, log(p/(1-p))), so two distinct probability points give an
   invertible linear system). State it as a short lemma; delete
   "likely". Conditions B and C are PWF-free given the (r, w1, w2)
   reparameterization, so this closes the GE case completely and lets
   footnote 9 in the main text point at an actual result.

4. BAYES FACTORS ARE STILL CONDITIONAL ON THE SHARED PRIOR.
   Section 5.4's framing ("a test of functional form, not of prior
   calibration") is right about CROSS-model contamination, but the BF
   magnitudes still depend on the single shared (direct prior, alpha,
   kappa) choice. The (alpha, kappa) sensitivity reruns exist only for
   Model 1. Either acknowledge this directly in the Discussion (one
   paragraph: the nested Prelec-vs-Power comparison is most robust;
   magnitudes are conditional on the shared prior) or, if cheap GPU time
   exists, recompute the three MLs at one alternative (alpha, kappa)
   corner to show the ordering is stable. The acknowledgment is
   sufficient; soften "clean measure of evidence" in the Conclusion
   accordingly.

## Minor comments

5. Notation: Section 3.4 and the Discussion say the decomposition
   applies to "each of the p predictive quantities" and "beliefs about
   p predictive quantities"; quantities are k (with k >= p). Use k or
   "each quantity".
6. Equation (9) chains two ":=" where the second should be "=".
7. Model 3 nesting: p.18 notes phi = 1 gives w(p) = p^eta; add "so the
   Power parameter delta corresponds to eta" to make the nesting map
   explicit (the BF section relies on it).
8. Section 5.4 refers to "the cross-model CE analysis of Section 5"
   from within Section 5; point to Section 5.1.
9. Appendix D.4 says "the two models recover fundamentally different
   parameter regions" where it means the two PRIORS (informative vs
   diffuse); after the three-model restructure this wording misleads.
10. Figures D.8/D.9 show small spurious spikes at the support edges
    (KDE boundary artifacts); trim the evaluation grid or note them.
11. Bibliography polish: Andersen et al. renders "(May 2008)" (legacy
    month field; biber warns); Eeckhoudt-Schlesinger now renders
    "(Mar. 2006)" for the same reason; Tanaka et al. title has a stray
    space before a colon; Kadane-Wolfson volume formatting is odd; biber
    reports four "possible runaway string" warnings in library.bib that
    are worth chasing (unbalanced quote/brace can silently truncate a
    field).
12. Length: 41 pages of main text plus 62 of appendices. For JoE,
    consider moving the Appendix D figure series (D.1-D.6) to an online
    supplement; the tables and D.7-D.9 carry the substance.
13. Table 2's "Implied Var." column reports dollar-squared values next
    to a probability variance (0.0090); consider units in the header or
    separating P into its own row block.
14. Abstract: consider stating the headline BF result in one sentence;
    the abstract currently promises the comparison but reports no
    finding.

## What is in good shape

The two-camp literature review is fair and well-cited; the variance
decomposition exposition (Sections 3.2-3.4) is genuinely clear; the
feasibility analysis in Appendix A is complete with honest KS-distance
reporting; the injectivity theorem structure (Conditions A/B/C,
Proposition B.2) is clean and the Condition B kernel-monotonicity proof
is correct as far as I can verify line by line; Appendix C's
analytic-variety argument is correct and the m-to-one pullback
discussion is careful; the marginal likelihood appendix describes a
validated, replicable procedure, and reporting both integration routes
with replicate-based SEs is exemplary practice.
