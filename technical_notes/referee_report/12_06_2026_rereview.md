## Verified in this pass

**Front matter and framing.** The abstract now states that the three
models share one utility function and differ only in probability
weighting, and it carries the headline finding (Prelec decisively
favored over Goldstein-Einhorn despite nearly identical predictive
CEs). The introduction roadmap, the Section 4.2 hand-off ("supports the
formal nested comparison summarized in Section 5.4"), and the Section
5.1 closing hand-off ("a Bayes factor for the two flexible models...
and summarizes the corresponding evidence on the Power form") are all
consistent with the demoted presentation.

**Section 5.4.** The truncation qualification paragraph is correct and
well placed: feasible masses 0.72 / 0.72 / 0.39, definitions of
$C_m(\gamma)$ and $Z_m$, the statement that Models 1 and 2 are
compared on fully normalized terms while Model 3's normalizer is
bounded. Table 4 is the two-row normalized table; arithmetic checks
exactly ($-5254.7 + 36.5 = -5218.2$; $-5271.3 + 37.0 = -5234.3$). The
Bayes factor paragraph centers 16.1 ($\pm$0.4) with the "empirically
interchangeable yet decisively separated" framing. The Model 3 prose
paragraph correctly reports the bound $\geq 107.4$ (and $\geq 91.3$ for
Model 2 over Model 3 via Appendix E.2), with the honest closing that
the $k > p$ case lacks a sharp point estimate of the margin.

**Discussion and conclusion.** The Limitations subsection carries all
three caveats: the Prelec/GE verdict is conditional on the stated
prior; the Power rejection is robust to prior perturbations but its
normalizer is a bound; and full normalization of over-determined
specifications is "a methodological problem the quantity-space
framework inherits rather than solves." The Extensions and Conclusion
text matches.

**Appendices.** Appendix A (three families, heatmaps, Beta-Beta
cross-moment derivation, feasibility bounds) reads cleanly. Appendix B
includes the new Goldstein-Einhorn two-point lemma (Lemma B.9, the
logit-affine argument) with bijectivity onto the order-compatible
region; the kernel-monotonicity proof (Lemma B.12) and the
shared-probability Proposition B.13 are intact. Appendix C citations
(Krantz and Parks; Mityagin) render. Appendix D's configuration table
matches Table 3 row for row (83/14/14, 76/14/21, 81/13/17). Appendix E
is consistent end to end: Table E.1, the R-hat quantification (14%,
23%, 8% above 1.1; hyperparameter maxima 1.15 / 1.25 / 1.10), the E.1
estimator description with the synthetic validation ($-72.38$,
recovered within 0.03 on both routes), and the E.2 feasible-mass
subsection with the Jensen bound at $t^* = 40$ giving
$\log Z_3 \geq -100.8$, matching the run output ($-100.792$,
replicates $-100.592 / -100.792$).

**References.** No missing or malformed entries; Manderson and Goudie
(2026) and the corrected Kadane-Wolfson entry render properly. No

## Remaining observations (no action taken)

1. The introduction (page 3) states "Because all three models express
   identical prior beliefs about observables, Bayes factors between
   them measure evidence about functional form rather than differences
   in prior calibration" without the feasible-set qualification. This
   was a deliberate choice in the demotion pass: Section 5.4 opens its
   qualification paragraph with "One qualification makes the
   shared-prior statement precise," which covers it, and front-matter
   stating the clean version of a claim whose fine print is developed
   in the body is standard practice. Recommendation: leave as is. A
   referee who reads far enough to notice the truncation subtlety will
   find the qualification exactly where the comparison is made.

2. Footnote 9 (Section 4.3) summarizes the Model 3 injectivity
   argument as monotonicity in $\delta$ plus the CRRA argument plus a
   numerical Gram rank check. This matches Appendix B.4 and the
   Section 5 text. No inconsistency, just noting it was checked.
